The DoRA Empirical Rank Inflation Phenomenon

Comparing effective rank of low-rank core $\Delta V = \frac{\alpha}{r}BA$ vs merged update $\Delta W = W' - W_0$

Key Theoretical Insight

Why does Rank Inflation occur in DoRA?

In vanilla LoRA, the effective update is strictly low-rank: $\Delta W_{\text{LoRA}} = \frac{\alpha}{r}BA$, meaning $\text{rank}(\Delta W) \le 16$. In DoRA, the merged weight is $W' = m \odot \frac{W_0 + \Delta V}{\|W_0 + \Delta V\|_c}$. When computing the effective change $\Delta W = W' - W_0$, the non-linear column-wise vector normalization ($L_2$ norm per column) couples the full-rank base weight $W_0$ with the low-rank delta. Consequently, $\Delta W$ is mathematically full-rank across all singular value spectra, yielding an effective entropy rank $r_{\text{eff}}(\Delta W) \approx \mathbf{800.5} - \mathbf{814.1}$ (an $83\times - 104\times$ expansion)!

Practical Application

Given that cross-domain sign conflict on top-5% magnitude coordinates is $40.55\%$ (nearly 10% below random interference chance of 50%), merging the Poetry and Prose adapters is geometrically viable. Recommended recipe:

  1. Magnitude Trimming (DARE/TIES): Drop bottom 80% coordinates ($\Delta W$) with low magnitude to eliminate high-entropy noise where interference peaks ($42.65\%$).
  2. Elect Sign Consensus: Resolve directional sign conflicts by voting on top-20% coordinate signs.
  3. Magnitude Scaling: Scale merged direction $V_{\text{merged}}$ with average magnitude vectors $m_{\text{avg}} = \frac{m_{\text{poetry}} + m_{\text{prose}}}{2}$.

Theoretical Foundations

Mathematical Formulations & Notation Reference

Formal mathematical definitions of all metrics implemented in the DoRA Geometric Analysis Suite.

1. DoRA Weight Decomposition

$$W' = m \odot \frac{W_0 + \frac{\alpha}{r} B A}{\left\|W_0 + \frac{\alpha}{r} B A\right\|_c}$$

Where $W_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}} \times d_{\text{in}}}$ is the frozen base model weight, $B \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}} \times r}$ and $A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d_{\text{in}}}$ are low-rank matrices ($r=16, \alpha=32$), and $m \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}}}$ is the learnable magnitude vector initialized to $\|W_0\|_c$.

2. Effective Rank ($r_{\text{eff}}$)

$$r_{\text{eff}}(M) = \exp\left(-\sum_{i=1}^k p_i \ln p_i\right), \quad p_i = \frac{\sigma_i}{\sum_j \sigma_j}$$

Entropy-based effective rank (Roy & Vetterli, 2007) of singular values $\sigma_i$. While $\Delta V = \frac{\alpha}{r}BA$ has strict rank $\le 16$, the merged update $\Delta W = W' - W_0$ achieves $r_{\text{eff}} \approx 800$ due to column normalization.

3. Magnitude-Gated TIES Sign Conflict

$$\text{Conflict}_K = \frac{1}{|S_K|} \sum_{(i,j) \in S_K} \mathbb{I}\left[\operatorname{sgn}(\Delta W_{ij}^{(1)}) \ne \operatorname{sgn}(\Delta W_{ij}^{(2)})\right]$$

Evaluated strictly over the top $K \in \{5\%, 10\%, 20\%\}$ coordinates $S_K$ with highest average absolute magnitude: $\frac{|\Delta W_{ij}^{(1)}| + |\Delta W_{ij}^{(2)}|}{2}$, benchmarked against the $50\%$ shuffled null chance baseline.

4. Grassmannian Subspace Overlap

$$\text{Overlap}(U_1, U_2) = \frac{1}{p} \sum_{i=1}^p \cos^2(\theta_i) = \frac{1}{p} \|U_1^T U_2\|_F^2$$

Projection metric on the Grassmann manifold $\mathcal{G}(p, d)$ over the top-$p=16$ left singular vectors ($U$, output semantic space) and right singular vectors ($V$, input contextual space).
